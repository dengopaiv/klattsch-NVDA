Klattsch Native

Version {VERSION}. {STATUS}

A formant speech synthesizer for NVDA. It is klattsch, Tony Gies's parallel-formant synthesizer, rewritten in C, with an English text front end in front of it. Everything it does is in one native library, so it needs no Python packages and no data files.

It needs 64-bit NVDA, 2026.1 or later. It speaks English only.

Choosing it

Press NVDA+Control+S to choose a synthesizer, and pick Klattsch Native. Its settings are then in NVDA's Voice settings (NVDA+Control+V).

Settings

NVDA's usual settings, plus the voice's own. The ones marked "ring" are also in the synth settings ring (NVDA+Control+arrows).

Rate (ring), Rate boost (ring)
How fast it speaks. Faster means shorter sounds, never a higher voice. Rate boost doubles the speed again.
Pitch (ring)
The voice's base pitch: an octave below 120 Hz at 0, an octave above at 100.
Inflection (ring)
How much the pitch moves over a sentence. 0 is a monotone; 100 is twice the normal movement.
Volume (ring)
Loudness.
Breathiness (ring)
Noise mixed into the voice. 0 is none.
Effort (ring)
The shape of each pulse of the voice: lax and soft below 50, tense and bright above.
Head size (ring)
Every resonance of the voice scaled together. Above 50 sounds like a smaller head, below 50 a larger one.
Spectral tilt
Darker below 50, brighter above.
Vibrato depth, Vibrato speed
A wavering pitch. Depth 0 is none.
Sample rate
22,050, 44,100 or 48,000 Hz. 22,050 is the default and is enough; lower rates are not offered, because below about 12 kHz the s sound collapses into sh.

What to expect

  • The voice is deliberately simple: three formants, as klattsch was designed. It is clear rather than natural.
  • Pronunciation comes from letter-to-sound rules, not a dictionary, so some words come out wrong. Stress is right for about four words in five.
  • The settings' ranges, and the sentence melody, are first versions. They will change after listening.
  • When NVDA places a reading position in the middle of a sentence, the sentence melody starts again from there.

Who made it

  • klattsch, the synthesizer, its phoneme language and its phoneme banks: Tony Gies. MIT licence, copyright 2026 Tony Gies. The C engine in this add-on is a translation of his JavaScript.
  • The English phoneme bank: from D. H. Klatt, "Software for a cascade/parallel formant synthesizer", JASA 67(3), 1980.
  • The letter-to-sound rules: Elovitz, Johnson, McHugh and Shore, NRL Report 7948, Naval Research Laboratory, 1976; arranged in C by John A. Wasser (1985, public domain).
  • The exception dictionary and three letter names: Tamas Geczy, from his votraxsc01 NVDA add-on. BSD-3-Clause.
  • The C rewrite, the text front end's stress, melody and number reading, and this add-on: Päiv Dengo. The front end is BSD-3-Clause; the rest is MIT.
